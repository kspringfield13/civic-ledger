# Legal and Ethical Boundaries

## Permitted
- Public records: procurement portals (e.g. USAspending, state checkbooks),
  inspector general reports, GAO reports, public court records (PACER/
  CourtListener), official enforcement releases, sanctions/debarment lists
  (SAM.gov exclusions), corporate registries where lawfully accessible.
- Open datasets published for reuse, respecting their licenses and terms.
- FOIA planning and drafting (requests are lawful public-records process).
- Data the operator provides with documented authorization to use.

## Prohibited — build nothing that enables these
- Unauthorized access of any kind: credentials not ours, bypassing auth,
  exploiting vulnerabilities, malware, or evasion of technical controls.
- Scraping private, authenticated, or ToS-prohibited systems.
- Phishing, impersonation, social engineering, pretexting.
- Privacy invasion: doxxing, tracking private individuals, aggregating
  personal data beyond what public-official/vendor accountability requires.
- Harassment, coercion, or publishing target lists of individuals.

## Accusation policy
System outputs are **risk signals** and **case leads** with confidence scores.
They are hypotheses for human review. No UI text, API response, export, or
document may state or imply that a person or entity committed fraud.
Escalation beyond the review queue (e.g. referral to an inspector general)
is a human decision made outside this system.

## Individuals vs. entities
Analysis centers on transactions and organizations. Named individuals appear
only in their official/registered capacities (contracting officers, registered
agents, listed principals) as recorded in public documents.

## When in doubt
Stop, document the question in the case notes, and ask the operator.
Legality is the floor, not the target: choose the more conservative option.
